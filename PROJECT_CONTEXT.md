# TWZ Manager — Project Context

## What this is

TWZ Manager is an internal web app (PWA) for **Two Wheels Zone**, a multi-branch motorcycle shop business. It digitizes the daily sales audit and bank deposit reconciliation process that is currently done by hand. The primary users are **branch managers**; the **business owner** uses it for oversight and assurance.

It ships as a PWA used on phones and laptops because the team cannot build a native mobile app yet.

## The problem

Today the process is traditional and handwritten:

1. Every night, each branch audits the day: they list everything sold and all expenses on paper.
2. They deposit the day's earnings to the bank.
3. They take a photo of the deposit receipt and send it to the owner.

This is slow, error-prone, and gives the owner no reliable way to verify that the amount deposited actually matches what the branch earned that day. The owner's core need is **assurance that deposits are accurate**.

## What already exists

All sales in every branch go through **Loyverse POS**. Every sale is already recorded there, so the system does not need manual sales entry — it needs to pull sales data from Loyverse and reconcile it against bank deposits.

## What we are building

An automated daily sales audit and deposit reconciliation system, per branch (store):

1. **Sales tracking** — Pull each branch's sales for the day from Loyverse POS, so sales can be monitored throughout the day.
2. **Expense logging** — Branches record daily expenses (food and any other expense). The company covers meals and snacks (merienda) for staff, so these are legitimate expenses that are **automatically deducted** from the amount expected to be deposited. Every expense entry requires a **receipt attachment**, except staff meals and merienda — those are company-covered and are logged without one.
3. **Expected deposit computation** — For each branch, per day:

   ```
   Expected deposit = Loyverse POS daily sales − approved expenses (meals, snacks, other logged expenses)
   ```

4. **Deposit recording** — The branch manager deposits earnings to the bank and records the deposit in the app (amount + deposit slip/receipt photo). This replaces sending receipt photos to the owner manually.
   - **Deposits are not always daily.** Sometimes a branch deposits every day; sometimes it batches and deposits every ~3 days. A single bank deposit can therefore cover **one or several audited days**.
   - When recording a deposit, the manager selects which day(s) it covers (typically all unreconciled days since the last deposit).
5. **Reconciliation (the core rule)** — The recorded bank deposit **must match** the total expected deposit for the day(s) it covers:

   ```
   Expected deposit (at deposit time) = sum over covered days of (POS daily sales − approved expenses)
   ```

   - **If it matches:** the covered days are closed as reconciled.
   - **If it does not match:** the app **automatically requires a discrepancy form** before the covered days can be closed. The form must have:
     - A **reason field** (with placeholder text guiding the manager on what to explain)
     - A **receipt attachment** field (e.g., meal receipts) to justify the difference
6. **Owner visibility** — The owner can see, per branch and per day: sales, expenses, expected deposit, actual deposit, match status, and any discrepancy forms with their attachments.

## Daily flow (target state)

| Step | Who | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Throughout the day | System | Sales flow into Loyverse POS; app tracks the running daily total per branch |
| During the day | Branch manager | Logs expenses (meals, snacks, others) with receipt photos; these auto-deduct from the expected deposit |
| Evening (every day) | Branch manager | Reviews and closes the day: POS sales total and logged expenses; app records the day's net income and adds it to the running amount due for deposit |
| Deposit day (daily, or batched up to every ~3 days) | Branch manager | Deposits the accumulated cash at the bank, then records the deposit amount, selects the day(s) it covers, and attaches the deposit slip photo |
| Deposit day | System | Compares deposit vs the total expected for the covered day(s). Match → those days reconciled. Mismatch → discrepancy form (reason + receipt attachment) is required |
| Anytime | Owner | Reviews dashboards/reports per branch: sales, expenses, amounts due for deposit, deposits, match status, discrepancies |

## Key rules

- The **audit is per branch (store), per day** — every day is closed with its POS sales and expenses, even when no deposit happens that day.
- **Deposits can be daily or batched** (commonly up to every 3 days). One deposit covers one or more audited days, and reconciliation runs against the **sum** of those days.
- Days that have been audited but not yet covered by a deposit show as **pending deposit** (amount due accumulates until the next deposit).
- A mismatch can never be silently closed — the discrepancy form (reason + attachment) is mandatory.
- Meals and merienda are company-covered: logged as expenses **without a receipt** and deducted automatically from the expected deposit.
- Receipts/attachments are required evidence for expenses (except meals and merienda), deposits, and discrepancies.

## Backend readiness

- **`src/lib/api/` is the seam.** `contracts.ts` states everything the app asks of a backend; `http.ts` implements it over our own API; `sample.ts` implements the same interface with the deterministic development data. `index.ts` picks between them from `VITE_DATA_SOURCE`, defaulting to **http** — a missing env var gives the real backend, so a production build cannot ship sample figures by accident. `src/lib/useApi.ts` is the read hook: loading, error, reload, and a guard that drops the result of a superseded request so a slow response for last week's range cannot land on top of this week's.
- **Contracts are range-shaped, never per-day.** The sample data could afford a function per day because it ran in memory; over a network that is a stampede, and against Loyverse's 300-requests-per-300-seconds *account* budget it is an outage. `dailySales(storeIds, {from, to})` returns one row per branch per day in a single call.
- **Days cross the wire as `YYYY-MM-DD` strings, not `Date`.** A Date is a UTC instant and a branch's audit day is a local calendar day; `dayKey()` (now in `dateRange.ts`, which no longer depends on the mock) formats from local parts rather than `toISOString()`, or every late-evening sale would key to the following day.
- **Credentials are an httpOnly cookie** (`credentials: "include"`), never a token in JS. `ApiError` carries the status and any field-level messages, and `client.ts` turns transport failures into "No connection" rather than a raw throw.
- Search is deliberately absent from the contract: it currently builds its index in the browser and cannot survive a real dataset, so the backend will own it as a query endpoint rather than inheriting the present shape.
- **Loyverse belongs to the backend alone** — `docs/LOYVERSE.md` has the study. The decisive fact: a Loyverse token has **no scopes at all** ("The token gives unlimited access to all resources provided by the Loyverse API"), so one in the browser would hand any manager full write access to the merchant's items, inventory, customers and refunds. Nothing in `src/lib/api/` mentions the POS; the frontend asks for daily sales and does not care where they came from.
- **`src/lib/mock.ts` is gone.** Every page, component and lib now reads through `api.*`; the sample figures live only in `src/lib/api/sample.ts`, behind the interface, and nothing outside that file knows the data is generated. Writes go to the adapter and the affected query is re-read, so the per-page override layers that used to hold them — `DayEdits` on Expenses, `branchOverride` on Managers, `recordedByStore`/`clearedByStore` on Deposits — are all deleted. A correction now survives navigating away, which it never did before.
  - `SessionProvider` resolves the identity and branch list once, before any page renders, so `useSession()` still hands back a non-null `manager` and `store` and no consumer had to grow a loading branch when the data moved behind the network.
  - `branchNotices()` and `buildCorpus()` are async and fire their independent queries with `Promise.all` rather than in sequence.
  - Verified end to end against the sample adapter: 4 branches × 14 days returns exactly 56 rows with `expected` reconciling to `gross − expenses`; an added expense survives a re-read, an update changes it and a delete removes it; recording a deposit closes exactly the days it covers (6 pending → 4) and records them on the deposit; a branch reassignment swaps the two managers and leaves one branch per manager.

## Technical notes

- **Frontend:** Vite + React 19 + TypeScript + Tailwind CSS v4. Icons: `@phosphor-icons/react`.
- **Motion:** GSAP + `@gsap/react`, with everything sequenced living in `src/lib/motion.ts` — the tokens (`EASE = "expo.out"`, which is the same `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` as the CSS `--ease-quiet`), the `prefers-reduced-motion` opt-out, and two shared hooks: `useRouteReveal` (one staggered fade-up over every `[data-rise]` in the shell's `<main>`, keyed on the path) and `useSheetEnter` (backdrop fade + panel rise on one timeline). The toast's card/ring/tick and the two popovers hold their own tweens. CSS keeps only hover and focus transitions. Every tween is transform + opacity, and the reveal clears its inline transform on land so nothing is left as a containing block.
- **Platform:** PWA (installable on phones; works on laptops).
- **POS integration:** Loyverse POS is the source of truth for sales. Integration will use the Loyverse API (stores, receipts) — exact endpoints/auth to be confirmed during backend work.
- **Auth:** Sign in with username or Gmail + password. Branch managers get accounts issued by the owner, each **assigned to exactly one branch**. A manager only ever sees and edits their own branch's data (sales, expenses, deposits, history); the branch is fixed by their account, never chosen in the UI. Only the owner (admin) area spans every branch. (Auth backend not built yet — the login form validates client-side only, and a mock `SessionProvider` resolves the typed identifier to one of the sample manager accounts to pick the acting branch.)
- **Repo:** https://github.com/sktle-niel/twz_manager (branches: `main`, `development`).

- **Cash advances are their own ledger, not an expense category.** An employee drawing against their pay takes real cash out of the drawer, so the day's `expected = profit − expenses − advances` — but the shop gets that money back, so it must never inflate what the branch *spent*. They are logged in a "Cash advances" card on the Expenses page (employee name + amount, list with per-row remove), scoped to the same selected day and frozen by the same closed-day rule as expenses once a deposit covers the day. On the wire: `GET/POST/PATCH/DELETE /advances`, `DayAudit.advances`, and the owner's ReceiptDialog shows a "Cash advances (deducted)" line so a smaller expected deposit explains itself. Employees are names, not accounts. Repayment tracking (who still owes what across paydays) is deliberately not in this slice — it is a receivables ledger for later, and recording the draw is what reconciliation needs today.
- **An expense of ₱10,000 or more pauses the batch save once** (`BIG_EXPENSE` in `ExpensesPage.tsx`): a modal lists the large lines ("₱90,000 for Electric bill"), says plainly that a slipped digit here is what turns a day's expected deposit negative, and asks "Is the amount right?" — confirming saves everything as typed, going back loses nothing. A confirm rather than a cap, because a genuinely large bill exists and must go through on one click.
- Expenses page validation: amount is required, the note is always optional, and the receipt photo is required for every category **except Meals and Merienda** (`RECEIPT_OPTIONAL` in `ExpensesPage.tsx`) — the field relabels itself "(optional)" and any stale receipt error clears the moment one of those two is picked. Categories: Meals, Merienda, Water bill, Electric bill, Wifi bill, Other — each with its own icon, note placeholder, and default note used when the note is left blank. In the mock, the three utility bills are monthly rather than daily: electric on the 5th, water on the 10th, wifi on the 15th of each month, per branch, noted with the billing period. They are seeded independently of the daily meal spend, and a day's items are sorted by time.
- The deposit slip can be **shot inside the app** (`src/components/SlipCamera.tsx`): a full-screen rear-camera preview with a frame guide, requested at 1920×1080 so the capture clears both the legibility floor and the resolution OCR wants. The reason to shoot here rather than hand off to the gallery is the **live guide** — `documentMetrics()`/`documentVerdict()`, the same measurements that judge a filed photo, run on the preview every 400ms and steer the manager ("Fill the frame with the slip", "Get closer so the printing shows", "Looks good — hold steady") instead of rejecting the shot afterwards. The shutter is never disabled on that reading: it is guidance, and a manager who needs the shot must always be able to take it. `getUserMedia` needs a secure context, so the failure paths — insecure origin, permission declined, no camera — each say which one happened and point at the gallery. Tracks are stopped on unmount. `PhotoAttach` grew an optional `onCapture` so the field offers "Take photo" alongside "From gallery" (and "Retake" once one is attached) without the component knowing anything about cameras.
- The deposit slip photo is **checked as it is attached** (`src/lib/slipCheck.ts`), so a bad one is caught while it can still be retaken rather than after it is filed as evidence. "Valid" is not one judgement, so it does not report one: `inspectSlip()` runs named checks and returns every finding, because "invalid" is useless to a manager who cannot see which part failed. It reads the picture, never the numbers on it — pulling the amount off a slip is OCR, needing a backend or a heavy in-browser model, and a wrong reading that silently passes is precisely the failure this app exists to prevent. The manager types the amount and the app checks *that* against the expected total.
  - **`fail` blocks, `warn` does not.** Blocking: the file will not decode, the short side is under 480px, **the exact same photo was already filed against another deposit**, the **frame contains no document at all**, or — folded in after OCR — the page carries **none of the BDO slip's own wording**. A dummy, a random picture, or some other printed page cannot be filed. Warning: focus, contrast, a slip that is hard to make out, and a file more than a week old. Refusing an upload over a wrong focus reading would strand a manager at 9pm with a readable slip.
  - **What the document check can and cannot do.** It rejects frames with no sheet, no printing, or no line structure — faces, landscapes, blank walls, flat colours, noise, foliage. It **cannot tell a bank slip from any other printed page** — that needs to read the words on it, which `bankVerdict` in `slipRead.ts` now does once a frame passes (see below) — and cannot detect a forged or edited one. Treat it as a filter against junk, not as proof the deposit is genuine.
  - `documentMetrics()` **locates the sheet before measuring it**, via bright low-saturation projections, and reports fill, ink, and row banding *inside* that region. Measuring the whole frame is what the first attempt did, and it rejected real slips: a slip on a dark table came back at 75% "ink" because the table is dark, and a small slip on the same table at 90% — both would have blocked a manager from filing a good deposit. Two other ideas died the same way, on measurement rather than opinion: Otsu **separability** looked like a document detector until a landscape scored 0.90 against a slip's 1.00, because Otsu always finds *some* best split; and an adaptive ink threshold always halves the frame, so every photograph returned ~50% ink and the number meant nothing. Ink is now a fixed luma.
  - Calibrated against 12 generated frames — five slip variants (full frame, on a dark desk, on a pale desk, small and distant, faint thermal) and seven non-documents. All 12 classify correctly with wide margins: slips land at fill 0.73–0.91 and banding 1.33–1.93 against floors of 0.42 and 0.25, while every non-document fails on coverage or ink outright. `documentMetrics()` and `documentVerdict()` are pure — pixels in, numbers out, no canvas — precisely so this can be re-run when the thresholds are next touched.
  - **Reuse is the check that matters most** — one slip photographed twice would defeat the reconciliation entirely. Each filed slip leaves a SHA-256 of its bytes plus a 64-bit dHash (row-gradient perceptual hash); an exact byte match blocks, and a Hamming distance ≤ 8 warns, which catches a re-saved or re-cropped copy. Verified: the same picture re-saved lands at distance 3, a different slip at 20; a missing or malformed hash returns `MAX_SAFE_INTEGER` so it can never read as a match. Session-only per branch until the backend stores fingerprints — that is the real home for this check, since a duplicate across devices or weeks cannot be seen from one browser tab.
  - Focus is the variance of the Laplacian over a **centre crop at native scale** — downscaling the whole frame first smooths away the very detail being measured, so a blurry photo would come back looking sharp. Composition and the hash use a separate 64×64 draw of the whole frame. Verified against synthetic frames: a sharp slip scores ~4470, the same slip blurred ~7, a blank desk ~0 contrast, against thresholds of 60/15/12. Real phone noise inflates the measure, so the floors sit low on purpose.
  - The `knownSlips` array feeding the check is memoised: a bare `?? []` fallback hands the inspection effect a fresh array on every render, which re-inspects the slip forever.
- **The slip's printed validation is read back into the form** (`src/lib/slipRead.ts`, Tesseract.js). The branches fill their slips in by hand and the bank's printer validates them, so this reads the *printed* part deliberately — it is what the bank actually processed rather than what the manager wrote, and handwriting is beyond Tesseract regardless. Amount, reference, and deposit date are offered back; each fills only a field the manager has not already typed into, is labelled "Read from the slip — check it", and stops saying so the moment it is edited. **The reading itself shows no panel**: an earlier "Read from the slip: Amount ₱80,000 / Reference not found / (48% confidence)" box surfaced raw OCR guesses that only demanded explaining, and the owner had it removed — the figures land in the fields, the words feed the BDO verdict, and the raw-text disclosure went with the panel.
  - **The expected total is deliberately not used to choose between candidate readings.** Letting it break ties would make OCR agree with the books by construction and bury the very discrepancies the reconciliation exists to surface. Candidates are scored on the slip's own evidence: keyword proximity (`amount`, `total`, `ref`, `trn`), centavos and thousands grouping as the marks of a money field rather than an account number, then size. Dates prefer the most recent plausible one, so a form-revision date printed on the stationery loses to the validation date.
  - Verified against six transcripts of the kind Tesseract returns, all six correct: a keyword-led validation line, a bare dot-matrix line with no keywords at all, a slip whose account number must not beat the amount, a day-first date, a slip carrying an old form-revision date, and unreadable noise. `__parseForTest()` exists so the parsing — the part that gets a figure wrong — can be re-run without the multi-megabyte download behind it.
  - **Cost and limits.** The import is dynamic, so the main bundle grew ~6 kB and Tesseract splits into its own chunk; the WASM core and English data (~5–7 MB) download on first use and are cached in IndexedDB, which means the first deposit of a fresh install needs network. Accuracy on faint dot-matrix over a printed form is fair, not certain — which is why every autofilled figure stays editable and carries its "Read from the slip. Check it." hint.
  - Preprocessing does the heavy lifting: upscale to a ~1600px long edge (Tesseract wants roughly 300dpi text), flatten to grey, then stretch contrast with the top and bottom 2% clipped so one glare spot or dark corner cannot flatten the rest.
  - **The words are also the identity check** (`bankVerdict` in `slipRead.ts`): the transcript is matched against the BDO Network Bank slip's own vocabulary — strong marks that name the bank or the form ("BDO", "Network Bank", "Transaction Slip") and the form's printed labels ("Payor", "Denomination", "Machine Validation", "Savings Acct", …). A strong mark plus 3 marks in all reads as the slip; 5 label marks do without the brand (glare can eat the header); generic receipt words ("total amount", "php") count but can never clear a bar alone, so a shop receipt cannot pass. **"None of the wording anywhere" is the one OCR finding allowed to `fail`** — it is folded into the slip report by `foldBank()` and blocks recording; traces of the wording, or a reader that could not download on first use, warn and go through, per the same stranded-manager reasoning as every other heuristic. Figures are never offered off a page judged not to be the slip (a grocery total autofilled into the amount would be worse than nothing), and the submit button waits while checks run so a fast hand cannot outrace the verdict.
  - **A sideways slip OCRs to noise**, so before the verdict stands the frame is turned 90°/270°/180° and read again, stopping at the first pass that reads as the slip — a real slip pays one pass, only a genuine non-slip pays four. Calibrated end-to-end against the real photographed slip (Tesseract at 33% confidence on a crumpled, angled shot still yields 5 marks) plus six controls: the same slip sideways and upside down (caught at 270°/180°, up to 9 marks), a synthetic grocery receipt (2 marks — blocked), an illustration, bare wood, and a flat colour (0 marks — blocked). All seven classify correctly; the harness bundles the real `bankVerdict` rather than a copy of it.
- **A mismatch opens the discrepancy form inline**, the moment the figures disagree rather than after a failed submit: the amount over or short, the expected total against what was deposited, a required reason (`REASON_MIN` = 12 characters, so "ok" and "n/a" cannot stand in for an explanation the owner reads weeks later) with a guiding placeholder, and optional supporting receipts through the shared `ReceiptUploader`. Recording is blocked until the reason is written, and the confirmation toast names the discrepancy rather than reporting a plain success.
- Deposits page UI: the "For deposit" list shows **every** audited day still uncovered by a deposit — derived from `pendingDepositDays()` in the mock rather than assuming a fixed 3-day window, so a branch that has not deposited in six days sees all six, and Deposits and History always agree. Past the usual batching window it flags the backlog ("6 days waiting"). Covered-day selection, live match/discrepancy indicator against the expected total, required amount, reference number, and deposit-slip photo; recent deposits with Matched/Discrepancy status. The sample timeline lives in one place, `DEPOSIT_TIMELINE` in `src/lib/mock.ts` (days 1–6 pending, 7–8 covered by one matched deposit, day 9 short by 180).
  - **The batching window is enforced, not advisory.** The owner's "deposit batching window" setting was a dial that only worded a backlog warning; recording a deposit over more days than the window now refuses — backend first (**422** with `fields.days`, the authority), sample mode identically, and the Deposits page says it early: a claret notice appears the moment the selection exceeds the window ("Unselect N days, or record this as more than one deposit"), and `validate()` blocks with the same words. The page enforces only once the real rule has loaded — blocking on the fallback value would refuse selections the owner's actual window allows.
  - **A deposit covering several days is presented as its batch, never as one day's figure.** The covering deposit's amount repeats on every covered audit row, so without context a six-day ₱25,813 deposit on a ₱6,664 day read as "over by ₱19,149". Each `DayAudit` now carries `depositCovers` (the whole span, never clipped to the queried range) and `depositExpected` (the sum the deposit was judged against, **frozen on the deposit row at recording** — stored, so the verdict cannot drift if figures are recomputed later). The History table's Deposited cell notes "6 days" under the amount, the ReceiptDialog shows "One deposit covers · 6 days", this day's expected AND the batch expected, with over/short judged on the batch; search shortfalls and the dashboard discrepancy notice use the same rule. A multi-day deposit from before `expected` was stored shows no over/short figure at all — no verdict beats a wrong one.
